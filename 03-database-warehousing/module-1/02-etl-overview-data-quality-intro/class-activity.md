# Activity: The data behind everyday apps

In this activity you will find real numbers for how much data the companies you use every day deal with, track each number back to where it came from, and use the five Vs to explain why that data is hard to handle. You have about 30 minutes. There's no right or wrong answer here, so follow whatever you find interesting.

## 1. Pick what to explore

Pick a company or service you use, from these or anywhere else:

- **Social media:** Snapchat, TikTok, Instagram
- **Streaming:** Twitch, Netflix, Spotify
- **E-commerce:** Amazon, Temu, Finn.no

Messaging apps, search engines, ride sharing and online games all generate a lot of data too.

How you explore is up to you. You could:

- **Go deep on one company,** and find several statistics about different parts of what it does.
- **Compare a few companies in the same area,** such as Snapchat, TikTok and Instagram, and see where their numbers are similar and where they differ.
- **Look back in time,** and find how one of your numbers has changed from five or ten years ago.

## 2. For each statistic

1. **Find a statistic.** Look for a number that says how much data a company generates, collects, sends or stores. "500 hours of video uploaded every minute" is the kind of thing you're after.
2. **Find the most credible source for it.** Popular statistics get copied from site to site, often without a date or a link. Follow the number back as far as you can, ideally to the company itself or to a research report. Note when it was published.
3. **Explain what the number means.** Say what is being counted, in what unit, and over what period of time. Check whether the number is about data being **generated**, **sent** or **stored**, because those can be very different amounts. A platform might record every click its users make but only keep a summary of them.
4. **Identify the Vs.** Decide which of the five Vs the statistic shows. Two is a good number to aim for, but some statistics only really show one.
5. **Explain the challenge.** Say why handling this data would be hard for the company. Think about storing it, processing it fast enough, keeping it accurate, or getting something useful out of it.

## 3. The five Vs

| V | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| **Volume** | How much data is there? |
| **Velocity** | How fast does it arrive, and how fast does it need to be processed? |
| **Variety** | How many different kinds of data are there - text, images, video, clicks, locations? |
| **Veracity** | How reliable and accurate is it? |
| **Value** | What can the company gain from it? |
